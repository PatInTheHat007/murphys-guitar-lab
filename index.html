How it works

From a new guitar to your first song.

Everything you need is in the free lesson app. Each step takes a few minutes.

  1. Tune up

    The built-in tuner listens to each string and tells you to tighten or loosen it.

  2. Learn two chords

    Em and G, shown as clear finger pictures, with a sound you can compare against.

  3. Play with the band

    Drums and bass keep time while you strum. That's your first song.

Your path

Five levels, from first chord to your own sound.

Each level ends with a short checklist. When you can do everything on it, you move up.

  1. 1

    Beginner

    About 2 months

    Open chords, strumming, your first songs and your first scale.

  2. 2

    Advanced beginner

    Months 2–4

    Power chords, the 12-bar blues, bends and fingerpicking.

  3. 3

    Intermediate

    Months 4–9

    Barre chords, keys, and soloing across the whole neck.

  4. 4

    Upper intermediate

    Months 9–18

    CAGED, modes, learning by ear and songwriting.

  5. 5

    Advanced

    18 months on

    Jazz harmony, advanced technique, recording and performing.

Practice tips

Four habits that make the difference.

01

Slow is fast

Play slowly enough that you never stop. Speed comes on its own.

02

Count your changes

Time one minute of chord switches. Try to beat it tomorrow.

03

End on a home note

Finish a tune on a note from the band's chord and it sounds finished.

04

Sore fingers are normal

Fingertips toughen up in two or three weeks. Little and often helps most.

Questions

Good to know

Do I need to read music?

No. Everything uses tab and chord pictures, and the lessons show you how to read them.

What guitar do I need?

Any acoustic, electric or nylon-string guitar works. Lesson 1 has a tuner that listens through your microphone. For help choosing a guitar and making it easier to play, see Gear and setup.

How long until I can play a song?

Most people can play their first song with the backing band within a week of practising 10 minutes a day.

My fingers hurt and my chords buzz. Is that normal?

Yes. Sore fingertips for the first few weeks, buzzing strings and slow chord changes happen to everyone. What's normal explains each one and what helps.

I already play. Where should I start?

Check the list at the end of each level in the full course. Start at the first level where you can't yet do everything on it.

How do online lessons work?

You join a video call from your computer, tablet or phone. You'll need a quiet room and a camera that shows your fretting hand. After each lesson you get a written practice plan and a recording.
