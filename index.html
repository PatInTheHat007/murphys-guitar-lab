The method

Music first. Map second. Speed last.

1. Two chords in time

Em to G (or Em to Asus2). Downstrokes. Change on beat 1. This is the skill that makes songs exist.

2. Two cells

On one string: 2 frets or 3 frets. Stack them into position 1 (A C D E G). Land on a dry stone — the chord tone — when the bar ends.

3. Same cells, new camera

3NPS and extra boxes wait until the two-chord change is steady at Walk 60. CAGED is a later photo album of the grips you already play.

Path

What you actually do in the lab

  1. Hands and nut. Fret 1 is the first space after the nut. Thin string on top of every picture.
  2. Em + Asus2, then Em–G. One-minute change drill before any pentatonic crossing.
  3. Four notes over Am–C. D–E and G–A. Chart with slashes, not numbered boxes.
  4. Stack position 1. Start A (low E 5). Land C (high E 8) on bar 2 beat 4. Last note is dry.
  5. Ear and mute. Say the letter. Mute strings that are not in the chord.
  6. 3NPS window only after Em–G holds time. Then two homes for C (E8 and A3).
  7. Cowboy set + C Am F G. Point at the root in the grip. That is enough CAGED for now.

Practice

Rules that keep people

One-minute changes

Timer. Em to G. Count clean changes. Tomorrow beat today’s number. This is the drill courses use because it works.

Dry on the last slash

Am bar: rest on A, C, or E. C bar: rest on C, E, or G. Walking tones are legal. Endings want a chair.

Do not collect boxes

Five pent “boxes” are the same two cells shifted. Learn the cells. Shift when position 1 is a room you know in the dark.

3NPS is a highway

Great for sequences and speed. Poor as week-one homework. Gate it: if the song falls apart, stay on chords.

FAQ

Which file do I open?

index.html is this page. murphy-guitar-lab.html is the lessons. Keep both. Gold buttons open the lab.

Why not CAGED first?

CAGED is five photos of chords. You need two chords in time first. We point at roots inside cowboy grips, then hang the full album later.

Why A minor pentatonic in C?

Same five letters as C major pentatonic. No sharps. One stack, two names.
