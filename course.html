Level 1 · Beginner

First songs

Get the guitar in tune, learn the open chords and play complete songs with a steady strum.

  • About 2 months
  • 10–15 min a day
  • Uses the lesson app

0 of 8 done

1.1Meet your guitarStrings, frets, finger numbers and tuning✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The string names, low to high: E A D G B E
  • How frets are numbered, and which finger is 1, 2, 3 and 4
  • How to tune with a tuner, and to check by ear with the 5th-fret method

Practice

  • Tune up every day before you play. Say each string name out loud as you pluck it.

You're ready when

  • You can tune all six strings in under two minutes.

In the app: Lesson 1

1.2Holding the guitar and pickPosture, thumb position, pick grip and the strumming motion✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Sitting position, with the guitar body resting on your leg and the neck tilted up a little
  • Thumb behind the neck, fingertips arched so they don't touch other strings
  • Holding the pick between the thumb and the side of your index finger, with just the tip showing
  • Strumming from the wrist rather than the elbow

Practice

  • One minute of slow down-strums on the open strings, keeping a relaxed wrist and an even volume.

You're ready when

  • You can strum for a minute without your shoulder tensing up or the pick slipping.

In the app: Lesson 2

1.3Reading tab and chord diagramsThe simple way guitarists write music down✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Tab: six lines are the strings, numbers are frets, and you read left to right
  • Chord diagrams: dots are fingers, O means play the string open, X means don't play it
e|-----------0-----|
B|---------0---1---|
G|-------0-------2-|
D|-----2-----------|
A|---2-------------|
E|-0---------------|

Practice

  • Play the line above slowly, then make up your own four-note line and write it in tab.

You're ready when

  • You can play any short tab without asking which string is which.

In the app: Lesson 3

1.4Em and G, and your first chord changesTwo chords and the one-minute challenge✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Em (two fingers) and G
  • Moving all your fingers to the next chord at once, rather than one at a time
  • Keeping a finger in place when two chords share it

Practice

  • The one-minute challenge: count your clean Em → G switches in 60 seconds, write the number down and try to beat it tomorrow.

You're ready when

  • You can make 30 or more clean changes in a minute.

In the app: Lessons 4–5

1.5Your first song and the down-up strumChord charts, counting to four and never stopping✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Reading a chord chart, where each slash is one beat
  • Counting "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &": down on the numbers, up on the "&"
  • Keeping your hand swinging even when you don't hit the strings
  • The pattern behind countless songs: D · D U · U D U

Practice

  • Em and G with the app's band, four beats per chord, at slow speed and then medium.

You're ready when

  • You can play the Em–G song all the way through without stopping, even if a change is messy.

In the app: Lessons 6–7

1.6Your first notes and the five-note scaleSingle notes, the minor pentatonic scale and the home note✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Playing single notes cleanly, one finger per fret
  • The A minor pentatonic "box" at the 5th fret, the shape behind most rock and blues solos
  • Ending phrases on the home note (A) so they sound finished

Practice

  • Three minutes of the scale up and down with a metronome at 60, then make up short tunes over the backing track.

You're ready when

  • You can play the box up and down from memory with no wrong notes.

In the app: Lessons 8–10

1.7The open chordsC, D, A, Am, E, Dm, plus major versus minor✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Six new chords. Together with Em and G, these are the nine open chords most songs use.
  • The difference by ear: major chords sound bright, minor chords sound darker
  • Easy pairs first: Am ↔ C, E ↔ Am, D ↔ G, A ↔ D

Practice

  • Do the one-minute challenge with a different pair each day.

You're ready when

  • You can play all nine chords from memory, with every string ringing clearly.

In the app: Lesson 11

1.8Four-chord songsC–G–Am–F family progressions, and changing chords in time✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The progressions behind hundreds of songs: G–D–Em–C and C–G–Am–F (an easy Fmaj7 for now)
  • The trick that makes changes sound smooth: lift your fingers on the last up-strum ("4 &") so you land on time
  • Playing songs from start to finish with the app's jam room

Practice

  • Two songs from the song list below, every day, from start to finish.

You're ready when

  • You can play three songs through at about 75 beats per minute without stopping.

In the app: Lesson 12 and the jam room

Songs for this level

  • Amazing Grace (traditional): G, C, D
  • Bad Moon Rising: D, A, G
  • Knockin' on Heaven's Door: G, D, Am, C
  • House of the Rising Sun (traditional): Am, C, D, Fmaj7, E
  • Any G–D–Em–C pop song you love

Common problems

  • Buzzing: move your finger closer to the fret, not harder onto the string.
  • Muted strings: arch your knuckles more and play on your fingertips.
  • Stopping at changes: slow down until you don't stop.

Ready for Level 2 when you can…

  • Play all nine open chords from memory
  • Change between any two of them in time at 75 beats per minute
  • Play three complete songs with a steady strum pattern
  • Play the pentatonic box up and down from memory

Level 2 · Advanced beginner

Rock, blues and fingerpicking

Power chords, the 12-bar blues, your first real solos and fingerstyle playing.

  • Months 2–4
  • 20 min a day

0 of 8 done

2.1Power chordsTwo-note shapes that move anywhere on the neck✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The power chord shape with its root on the 6th string and on the 5th string
  • Naming them by the root note, such as G5 at the 3rd fret and A5 at the 5th
  • Muting unused strings with the underside of your first finger
   G5    A5    C5    D5
D|-5-----7-----5-----7---|
A|-5-----7-----3-----5---|
E|-3-----5---------------|

Practice

  • Move the shape along the neck with a metronome, changing on every beat.

You're ready when

  • You can play a rock progression with power chords and no ringing open strings.
2.2Palm muting and driving eighth notesThe chug behind rock rhythm guitar✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Resting the side of your picking hand lightly on the strings right at the bridge
  • Steady down-picked eighth notes
  • Accents: switching between muted and open strums to build energy

Practice

  • Two minutes of muted eighths on E5 at 80, then 100, then 120 beats per minute.

You're ready when

  • You can keep even eighths for a full minute at 110 beats per minute.
2.3The 12-bar bluesThe shuffle rhythm and the form behind blues, rock and roll and more✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The 12-bar form in A: four bars of A, two of D, two of A, then E, D, A, E
  • The classic shuffle riff, rocking between the 5th and 6th of each chord
  • The shuffle (swing) feel: long-short, long-short
   A5 shuffle (repeat)
D|-2-2-4-4-2-2-4-4-|
A|-0-0-0-0-0-0-0-0-|

Practice

  • Play three choruses (36 bars) over a blues backing track without losing your place.

You're ready when

  • You always know which bar you're on, and you change chords without looking.
2.4Reading rhythmQuarters, eighths, sixteenths, rests and ties✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The counts: "1 2 3 4", "1 & 2 &", and "1 e & a"
  • Rests (silence that still counts as time) and ties (holding a note over the beat)
  • Clapping a rhythm before you play it

Practice

  • Clap, then strum, one written rhythm a day on a single chord.

You're ready when

  • You can read a one-line rhythm and play it correctly the first time at a slow tempo.
2.5Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slidesMaking notes flow together✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Hammer-on (h): pick one note, then slam a finger down to sound the next
  • Pull-off (p): flick the finger off sideways to sound the lower note
  • Slide (/ or \): keep pressure on while you glide to a new fret
G|-5h7-7p5-----5/7-7\5-|
D|---------7-----------|

Practice

  • Go through the pentatonic box using only hammer-ons on the way up and pull-offs on the way down.

You're ready when

  • Hammered and pulled notes are as loud as the picked ones.
2.6Bends, vibrato and your first soloMaking the guitar sing✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Whole-step bends on the G and B strings, using three fingers together and pushing with your wrist
  • Bending in tune: check that the bent note matches the note two frets up
  • Vibrato: small, even, repeated bends
  • Building a 12-bar solo from short licks with space between them

Practice

  • Check ten bends a day against the target note. Improvise over a blues track for five minutes.

You're ready when

  • You can improvise a full 12-bar solo with bends that land in tune.
2.7FingerpickingPlaying the strings one at a time with your fingers✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Finger assignments: the thumb plays the bass strings, and the index, middle and ring fingers play the G, B and high E strings
  • Picking each chord one string at a time (arpeggios)
  • An alternating thumb bass, the starting point of Travis picking
   C
e|-------0-------0-|
B|---1-------1-----|
G|-----0-------0---|
D|-----------------|
A|-3-------3-------|

Practice

  • Five minutes of fingerpicking a C–Am–F–G loop.

You're ready when

  • You can fingerpick a whole song with a steady thumb that never stops.
2.8The F chord, capo and colour chordsYour first barre, plus sus and add chords✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The small F barre on the top four strings, as a step toward full barre chords
  • Using a capo to play songs in a different key with the shapes you already know
  • Colour chords: Dsus2, Dsus4, Asus2, Cadd9, used to add movement to strums

Practice

  • Do the one-minute challenge: C ↔ F.

You're ready when

  • F rings clearly and you can change C → F in time.

Songs for this level

  • Wild Thing: A, D, E with power chords
  • Famous single-note riffs, such as Seven Nation Army and Smoke on the Water
  • Any 12-bar blues, with shuffle rhythm and a solo
  • Scarborough Fair (traditional), fingerpicked

Common problems

  • Flat bends: check them against the target note every time.
  • F won't ring: roll your first finger slightly onto its bony edge.
  • Uneven fingerpicking: get the thumb steady first, then add the fingers.

Ready for Level 3 when you can…

  • Play a 12-bar blues shuffle for three choruses, then solo over it with bends and vibrato
  • Play power chords cleanly with palm muting at 110 beats per minute
  • Fingerpick a complete song
  • Play F and change to it in time

Level 3 · Intermediate

The whole neck

Barre chords, the notes on the neck, how keys work and soloing in every position.

  • Months 4–9
  • 30 min a day

0 of 8 done

3.1E-shape barre chordsRoot on the 6th string: major, minor and 7✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The full barre: your first finger acts as a capo, with the E shape played by the other fingers
  • Major, minor and dominant 7 versions
  • Using your arm's weight to press, rather than squeezing with your thumb

Practice

  • Slide F → G → A along the neck. Then play the minor versions.

You're ready when

  • All six strings ring, and you can hold the chord for four bars without pain.
3.2A-shape barre chordsRoot on the 5th string, so you can play any chord in two places✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The A-shape major (with a small ring-finger barre) and minor
  • Finding any chord with its root on the 6th or 5th string
  • Choosing the closest shape so you barely have to move

Practice

  • Play I–IV–V progressions in five keys, using only barre chords.

You're ready when

  • You can play any major or minor chord in two places within two seconds.
3.3Notes on the neckLearning the low E and A strings, and octave shapes✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The musical alphabet and where the half steps fall: B–C and E–F have no sharp between them
  • Landmark frets: 3, 5, 7, 12
  • Octave shapes that copy any note to another string

Practice

  • Daily drill: pick a random note and find it on every string within ten seconds.

You're ready when

  • You can name any note on the 6th and 5th strings instantly.
3.4Keys and the major scaleWhy songs use the chords they do, and the number system✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The major scale pattern: whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half
  • The chords in a key: I ii iii IV V vi vii°. In G that's G, Am, Bm, C, D, Em, F#°.
  • The Nashville number system: "1–5–6–4" works in any key
  • Finding the key of a song by ear and from its chords

Practice

  • Write out the chords in the keys of C, G, D, A and E. Move one known song into a new key.

You're ready when

  • You can move any song into a new key without writing it down.
3.5All five pentatonic positionsConnecting the scale across the whole neck, plus the blues scale✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Positions 1–5 of minor pentatonic, and how each one overlaps the next
  • Sliding between positions on a single string
  • The blues scale: pentatonic plus the ♭5 "blue note"
  • Major pentatonic is the same shapes, starting from a different root

Practice

  • Learn one new position a week. Then improvise while moving through all five over one backing track.

You're ready when

  • You can solo from the nut to the 17th fret without getting lost.
3.6Solos that sound like musicPhrasing, space and targeting the chord✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Call and response: play a phrase, then answer it
  • Using space: rests are part of the solo
  • Landing on the root or third of the current chord when the chord changes
  • Repeating and developing one idea instead of playing fifty

Practice

  • Solo with a limit: one string only, or three notes only, or one lick played five different ways.

You're ready when

  • A recording of your solo has a beginning, a middle and an end.
3.7Sixteenth-note strumming and syncopationGroove, muted chucks and off-beat accents✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Keeping your hand moving in sixteenths, "1 e & a", and choosing which strokes hit the strings
  • Percussive "chucks": hitting the strings while your fretting hand mutes them
  • Accenting the off-beats to create groove

Practice

  • Play one syncopated pattern a day with a drum loop and record it. Check that it locks with the drums.

You're ready when

  • You can copy a strum pattern from a recording by ear.
3.8Seventh chordsDominant 7, major 7 and minor 7, open and barre✓ Done

You'll learn

  • How each one sounds: bluesy (7), dreamy (maj7), smooth (m7)
  • Open shapes (A7, E7, D7, Cmaj7, Am7) and barre versions with the E and A shapes
  • Swapping 7th chords into songs you know to change their colour

Practice

  • Play a 12-bar blues with only 7th chords, then a slow song with maj7 and m7.

You're ready when

  • You can hear the difference between the three types without looking.

Repertoire goals

  • Two songs that use barre chords all the way through
  • A blues solo using at least three pentatonic positions
  • A funk or pop song with sixteenth-note strumming
  • One song moved into a new key for a singer

Common problems

  • Barre fatigue: squeeze less and pull back gently with your arm. Take breaks.
  • Stuck in the box: force yourself to start licks in positions 2–5.
  • Theory overload: use each idea in a song the same day you learn it.

Ready for Level 4 when you can…

  • Play any major, minor or 7th chord in at least two positions
  • Name the notes on the 6th and 5th strings instantly
  • Solo across all five pentatonic positions over a blues
  • Move a song into any key using numbers

Level 4 · Upper intermediate

Musicianship

Seeing the whole neck as one map, soloing with chord tones, modes, and learning by ear.

  • Months 9–18
  • 45 min a day

0 of 8 done

4.1The CAGED systemFive chord shapes that map the entire neck✓ Done

You'll learn

  • How the C, A, G, E and D shapes link up the neck for any chord
  • Playing one chord, such as G, in all five places
  • Linking each CAGED shape to its pentatonic position

Practice

  • Each day, pick one chord and play it in all five shapes, going up and then down the neck.

You're ready when

  • You can play any major chord in five positions.
4.2Triads and inversionsSmall three-note chords for rhythm parts that sit well in a band✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Major and minor triads on the top three strings, in all three inversions
  • Then the same on the middle string sets
  • Voice leading: moving to the nearest note of the next chord

Practice

  • Play a I–IV–V progression on the top three strings without moving more than two frets.

You're ready when

  • You can play a second guitar part that doesn't clash with the first.
4.3Arpeggios and chord-tone soloingFollowing the chords instead of only the key✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Major, minor and dominant 7 arpeggios, built from the CAGED shapes
  • Aiming for the 3rd of each chord as it arrives
  • Mixing pentatonic licks with arpeggio notes

Practice

  • Solo over a slow I–IV–V and land on the 3rd at every chord change.

You're ready when

  • Someone listening can hear the chord changes in your solo alone.
4.4Modes you'll actually useDorian, Mixolydian and the major and minor scales✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Each mode as a change to a scale you already know, not as a new shape
  • Dorian: minor with a bright 6th, heard in funk, soul and Santana-style playing
  • Mixolydian: major with a ♭7, heard in classic rock, blues and country
  • Choosing a mode from the chords: for a vamp of Am7 to D, play A Dorian

Practice

  • Improvise over one-chord vamps, switching between minor, Dorian and Mixolydian.

You're ready when

  • You can hear which mode fits a backing track and use it straight away.
4.5Picking technique and speedAlternate picking, economy picking and string skipping✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Strict alternate picking, and how to change strings cleanly
  • Economy picking: sweeping the pick through when you change string
  • The speed ladder: play clean at a tempo, raise it by 4 bpm, and go back down when it gets sloppy
  • Muting with both hands so fast lines stay clean

Practice

  • Ten minutes of speed ladder on scales and one lick. Log your clean top tempo every day.

You're ready when

  • You can play sixteenth notes cleanly at 100 bpm or faster.
4.6Funk, R&B and reggae rhythmNinth chords, scratching and the skank✓ Done

You'll learn

  • The E9 "funk chord" and small partial chords high on the neck
  • Sixteenth-note scratching, with a constant hand motion and muted strings
  • Double-stops and R&B fills
  • The reggae skank: short chords on the off-beats

Practice

  • Lock in with a drum loop for five minutes, and stay on one chord the whole time.

You're ready when

  • You can groove on one chord for a full song and it still sounds good.
4.7Ear training and transcriptionLearning songs and solos without tab✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Recognizing intervals with song references, such as the first two notes of a familiar tune
  • Finding the key first, then the bass notes, then the chords
  • Slowing a recording down without changing its pitch, and looping short sections

Practice

  • Fifteen minutes a day transcribing. Start with bass lines and simple riffs.

You're ready when

  • You've learned a whole solo by ear and can play it along with the recording.
4.8Songwriting and arrangingRiffs, progressions and song form✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Song sections: verse, chorus, bridge, and how to make each one feel different
  • Borrowed chords for surprise, such as ♭VII and iv in a major key
  • Writing riffs from scale fragments and rhythm ideas
  • Arranging: who plays where, and leaving room for the singer

Practice

  • Write one riff a day and record it on your phone. Finish one full song this level.

You're ready when

  • You have one original song recorded from start to finish.

Repertoire goals

  • One solo learned by ear, note for note
  • A rhythm part using triads, not full chords
  • A funk groove and a reggae groove
  • One original song

Common problems

  • Modes feel abstract: always play them over a backing chord. The chord is what makes the mode sound.
  • Speed plateau: slow down by 20%, relax, and focus on making smaller movements.
  • Transcription is slow: it's supposed to be. That slowness is how your ear learns.

Ready for Level 5 when you can…

  • Play any chord in five positions and triads on two string sets
  • Solo over chord changes, landing on chord tones
  • Hear and use Dorian and Mixolydian
  • Learn a solo by ear and write and record an original song

Level 5 · Advanced

Your own voice

Jazz harmony, advanced technique, solo arrangements, recording and performing.

  • Month 18 onward
  • 45–60 min a day

0 of 8 done

5.1Extended and altered chords9ths, 11ths, 13ths, m7♭5, dim7 and drop-2 voicings✓ Done

You'll learn

  • How extensions are built by stacking thirds past the octave
  • Which notes to drop so the chord fits under four fingers: usually the 5th, and sometimes the root
  • Drop-2 voicings on the middle four strings

Practice

  • Play a known song again using only extended voicings.

You're ready when

  • You can read a jazz or neo-soul chord chart and voice every chord.
5.2The ii–V–IThe most common progression in jazz, in major and minor keys✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Major ii–V–I (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7) and minor ii–V–i (Bm7♭5–E7–Am)
  • Comping: rhythmic chord accompaniment that leaves space for the soloist
  • Walking bass lines with chords on top, played on one guitar

Practice

  • Play ii–V–Is around the cycle of fourths, in two positions.

You're ready when

  • You can comp through a jazz standard's chord changes at a medium tempo.
5.3Soloing over changesMelodic and harmonic minor, and creating tension and release✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Harmonic minor over the V7 chord in minor keys
  • Melodic minor and the "altered" sound over V7
  • Chromatic approach notes and enclosures (circling a target note)
  • Guide-tone lines: connecting the 3rds and 7ths through the changes

Practice

  • Write a guide-tone melody over a standard, then improvise around it.

You're ready when

  • Your solos clearly follow the chord changes without leaning on a backing track to carry the harmony.
5.4Advanced techniquesLegato, tapping, sweep picking and hybrid picking✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Legato runs: three notes per string with hammer-ons and pull-offs
  • Two-hand tapping
  • Sweep-picked arpeggios across three and then five strings
  • Hybrid picking (pick plus fingers) for country and chicken-pickin' sounds

Practice

  • One technique per week, ten minutes a day, on the speed ladder.

You're ready when

  • You can use each technique musically in a solo, not only as an exercise.
5.5Advanced rhythm and feelOdd meters, swing, half-time and playing ahead of or behind the beat✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Counting and grooving in 5/4, 7/8 and 6/8
  • Swing versus straight time, and moving smoothly between them
  • Placing notes slightly ahead of or behind the beat to change the feel
  • Simple polyrhythms: three over two, four over three

Practice

  • Set the metronome to click only on beats 2 and 4, then only once per bar.

You're ready when

  • You can keep solid time with a click that plays only once every two bars.
5.6Solo guitar arrangingMelody, bass and chords together, plus alternate tunings✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Putting the melody on top and the bass on the bottom, with chord fragments filling the middle
  • Choosing a key that suits the guitar, so open strings help you
  • Alternate tunings: DADGAD, open D and open G

Practice

  • Arrange one traditional song, such as Amazing Grace, for solo guitar.

You're ready when

  • You can play a complete solo arrangement that needs no singer.
5.7Recording at homeInterface, mics, amp sims, levels and a clean mix✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Signal chain: guitar → audio interface → DAW (recording software), plus monitoring without delay
  • Gain staging: recording peaks around −12 dB, never clipping
  • Mic placement for acoustic guitar (around the 12th fret) and for an amp (near the edge of the speaker cone)
  • Recording direct (DI) and re-amping with amp simulators
  • Double-tracking, panning and basic EQ and compression

Practice

  • Record a full song: rhythm parts doubled and panned, a lead part, and a simple mix.

You're ready when

  • You can produce a clean demo you'd be happy to share.
5.8Performance and personal stylePlaying for people and sounding like yourself✓ Done

You'll learn

  • Building a set list: pacing, keys and transitions
  • Handling nerves: preparing so well you could play in your sleep, and recovering from mistakes without stopping
  • Gear for live playing: a simple, reliable signal chain
  • Finding your style: copy three players you love, then combine them

Practice

  • Run your full set once a week as a mock gig, standing up, without stopping.

You're ready when

  • You've played a 20-minute set for an audience, whether at an open mic, on a livestream or for friends.

Capstone projects

  • A 20-minute live set
  • A solo guitar arrangement
  • A recorded and mixed demo of an original song
  • Comping and soloing over one jazz standard

After this

  • Specialize in a style: jazz, blues, metal, fingerstyle, country or worship
  • Play with other people as often as you can
  • Teach a beginner. It shows you what you really understand.
